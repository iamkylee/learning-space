Design Thinking · Double Diamond

設計思考學習手冊：
用一個心理健康聊天機器人走完雙鑽石

整理自課堂簡報，補上外部方法與工具。全篇用同一個案例「晚安小站」串起四個階段，看一個模糊的問題如何一步步變成可測試的方案。不熟的名詞可以查名詞簡表。

00 · 總覽

從 A（不知道）走到 B（知道）

IDEO 執行長 Tim Brown 把設計思考定義為以人為本的創新方法：整合人的需求、技術的可能性與商業成功的條件。雙鑽石（Double Diamond）是英國設計協會（Design Council）2004 年提出的流程圖，把這件事拆成兩顆鑽石、四個階段，每一階段都先發散（打開可能性）再收斂（做出選擇）。

第一顆鑽石是做對的事（Designing the right thing）：弄清楚真正的問題，最後收斂成一個「我們可以如何……？」（How Might We，簡稱 HMW）問題。第二顆是把事做對（Designing things right）：找出並打磨解法。點下面的區塊可以跳到該階段。

底部標示每個交界處交出去的成果。在第一顆鑽石內部，研究資料依序被壓縮成 Insights → Themes → Opportunity areas → HMW。

簡報裡每一步都指定一種顏色。牆上一眼就能看出團隊走到哪一步，也能追溯每個點子來自哪個痛點。

案例設定：「晚安小站」

CASE · 貫穿全篇（虛構示範）

某大學諮商中心想做一個對話式代理人（conversational agent），陪伴學生處理日常情緒。初始需求只有一句話：「學生不太來諮商，我們想做個 AI（人工智慧，Artificial Intelligence）聊天機器人。」這就是 A 點，一份模糊的 client brief。

課堂引用 Tseng & Liao（2025）對心理健康對話代理人的四項設計洞察：結構化支持、情緒覺察與自我揭露、情緒安全與個人化、從追蹤到反思。這屬於次級研究（secondary research），後面會看到它們如何和團隊自己的發現互相印證。

01 DISCOVER 課堂 20 分鐘

探索：先理解人，不急著解題

目標：建立對人們心理健康經驗的豐富理解。產出：探索其中的張力與模式（Tensions / Patterns）。

課堂簡報上的三個動作
  • 分享田野故事：每人講自己訪談或觀察到的真實片段，越具體越好。
  • 找出關係，畫出張力與模式：哪些故事重複出現？哪裡有矛盾？
  • 關鍵字寫在紅色便利貼：一張一個概念，方便後續移動分群。
補充課堂沒展開、但這階段常用的方法
拆解題目（Rip the brief）

把 client brief 裡的假設逐條拆開。「學生不來諮商」是真的嗎？「AI 聊天機器人」是解法還是預設答案？

初級研究

深度訪談、情境觀察、日記研究。問「上次……是什麼情況？」，問具體經驗，少問意見。

次級研究

文獻、競品分析、統計資料。Tseng & Liao 的四項洞察就屬於這類。

同理心地圖

把一位使用者的 說／想／做／感受 分四格整理，找出說與做不一致的地方，那常是張力所在。

案例晚安小站的田野故事
CASE · DISCOVER

團隊訪談了 8 位學生、2 位諮商師。幾段代表性的故事（示範內容）：

  • 大三學生小安：凌晨兩點睡不著，想找人說話，但「這時間傳訊息給朋友好像很打擾」。
  • 研究生阿哲：下載過心情紀錄 App，每天點表情點了一個月，「圖表很漂亮，但我還是不知道自己怎麼了」。
  • 諮商師：初談預約要等兩到三週，「很多學生等到真的崩了才來」。
  • 大一學生 Mia：試過 AI 聊天，「它一直叫我深呼吸，感覺被敷衍」。

寫成紅色便利貼的關鍵字：

深夜最難熬
怕打擾朋友
怕被評價
諮商要排隊
紀錄了卻看不懂
罐頭回應＝被敷衍
說不出自己的感受
想要有人主動問

把便利貼放在一起看，浮現三組張力：

想被理解、想說出來
VS
怕被看見、怕被評價
希望有人引導、給方向
VS
討厭被說教、被套公式
記錄了很多情緒資料
VS
看不出意義，無法反思

這三組張力分別對應 Tseng & Liao 的「情緒安全與自我揭露」「結構化支持與個人化」「從追蹤到反思」。初級與次級研究互相印證，代表方向可信。

02 DEFINE 課堂 10 + 10 分鐘 + HMW

定義：把發現收斂成一個值得解的問題

課堂用三個步驟收斂：先標出痛點，再追問原因，最後改寫成 HMW 問題。對應流程圖裡 Insights → Themes → Opportunity areas → HMW 的壓縮過程。

課堂步驟一：痛點地圖（10 分鐘）

在使用者經歷的各個「時刻」貼上表情：😊 正向時刻（愉悅、順暢、成功）、😣 負向時刻（困惑、等待、挫折、不信任）。重點是找出那些需求沒被滿足的時刻。

補充：這其實就是簡化版的使用者旅程地圖（Customer Journey Map）。完整版會在每個時刻記錄行為、想法、情緒、接觸點與機會。

CASE · 小安的一夜

值得注意的是：AI 「隨時在、沒壓力」本身是正向時刻，問題出在回應品質和事後的意義感。這提醒團隊別把機器人整個推翻。

課堂步驟二：框定挑戰（10 分鐘，綠色便利貼）

針對每個負向痛點追問「為什麼」，寫成句型：

挑戰在於【人】難以【未被滿足的需求／問題】，因為【根本原因】。

補充：追問原因可用 5 Whys，連問五次「為什麼」，直到碰到可以著力的原因，而不是停在表面症狀。

CASE · 從「收到罐頭建議 😣」往下挖
  • 為什麼不信任？→ 回應跟自己說的內容無關。
  • 為什麼無關？→ 機器人沒抓到小安真正的情緒，只抓到「睡不著」。
  • 為什麼沒抓到？→ 小安自己也說不清楚，只講了表層。
  • 為什麼說不清楚？→ 平常沒有練習辨識情緒的語彙，也怕說太多被評價。
挑戰在於大學生難以在情緒低落的當下說出真正的感受，因為他們缺乏辨識情緒的語彙，而且不確定對方能否安全地承接。
挑戰在於學生難以從情緒紀錄中得到幫助，因為工具只呈現數據，沒有引導他們連結事件與感受。
課堂步驟三：寫出 HMW 問題（藍色便利貼）
我們可以如何幫助【人／利害關係人】【滿足需求／達成期望結果】，透過處理【根本原因】？

HMW 句型源自 1970 年代的寶僑（Procter & Gamble，P&G），後由 IDEO 發揚。每個字都有用意：How 代表可以解，Might 代表允許多種可能，We 代表一起做。

常見錯誤：範圍抓得太大或太小。

範圍例子問題
太大HMW 解決大學生的心理健康問題？不知從何下手
太小HMW 讓聊天機器人多加十種情緒按鈕？已經預設了解法
剛好見下方案例有方向，又留下多種解法的空間
CASE · 晚安小站的 HMW
HMW 幫助深夜睡不著的大學生安心地整理並說出自己的感受，透過處理他們缺乏情緒語彙、又擔心被評價的問題？
HMW 幫助有記錄習慣的學生從情緒紀錄中看見自己的模式，透過處理工具只給數據、不給意義的問題？

第一顆鑽石到此結束。團隊從「做個 AI 聊天機器人」走到兩個具體、以人為中心的問題，這就是交給第二顆鑽石的 Final brief。

03 DEVELOP 課堂 15 分鐘

發展：先求多，再分群

課堂兩個動作
  • 快速發想：一張黃色便利貼寫一個點子，針對 HMW 盡量多寫。
  • 把相似點子歸成較大的概念主題：用大張黃色便利貼當主題標題。這就是親和圖（Affinity Diagram）。
補充IDEO.org 的七條腦力激盪規則
1. 暫緩評判
現在不說「這不可能」。
2. 鼓勵瘋狂點子
離譜的點子常能引出可行的版本。
3. 接續別人的點子
用「好，而且……」取代「但是」。
4. 聚焦主題
把 HMW 貼在牆上隨時看。
5. 一次一人說話
每個想法都被聽見。
6. 視覺化
能畫就畫，比文字傳達更快。
7. 追求數量
15 分鐘的目標可以設 50 個點子。
延伸：Crazy 8s
8 分鐘畫 8 個草圖，逼自己跳出第一個想法。
CASE · 發想與分群

針對兩個 HMW，團隊 15 分鐘寫出 40 多張黃色便利貼，節錄如下，並分成四個主題：

主題 A：安心說出口對應「情緒安全與自我揭露」
匿名、不存對話的「樹洞模式」
機器人先分享一個情境，降低開口門檻
可以只丟一個顏色或貼圖代表心情
主題 B：有結構的陪伴對應「結構化支持」
「睡前三分鐘」固定三題引導
情緒詞卡：從「不舒服」細分到「委屈、焦慮、失落」
隨時說「我只想被聽」來關閉建議
主題 C：從紀錄到看見自己對應「從追蹤到反思」
每週一封「給自己的信」摘要
把情緒和事件連起來的時間軸
AI 提問：「這週哪一刻最像你？」
主題 D：接上真人補足 AI 的界線
偵測高風險字詞，提供校內與 24 小時專線
一鍵把整理好的摘要帶去初談
（瘋狂點子）AI 幫你寫訊息約朋友吃宵夜

四個主題中有三個和 Tseng & Liao 的洞察重疊；主題 D 是團隊從諮商師訪談中長出來的，反映了心理健康領域特有的安全需求。

04 DELIVER 評估 → 原型 → 測試 → 迭代

交付：選出值得做的，做出來，拿去測

課堂四個評估面向
可欲性 Desirability

真的滿足使用者需求、創造價值嗎？

可行性 Feasibility

現有資源與技術做得出來嗎？

存續性 Viability

對組織或生態系來說能長久維持嗎？

創新性 Innovation

有帶來新觀點或替代做法嗎？

補充：前三項是 IDEO 經典的「可欲性、可行性、存續性」（Desirability, Feasibility, Viability，簡稱 DVF）三圈，最好的方案落在三圈交集。課堂多加了「創新性」。設計思考建議先從可欲性出發，再引入另外兩個視角。

CASE · 互動評分（拖動滑桿試試）

團隊把四個主題濃縮成三個概念，每個面向 1–5 分。數值是示範，你可以依自己的判斷調整。

補充原型與測試：Build, test, iterate

流程圖第二顆鑽石的右半邊畫了好幾輪「建造、測試、迭代」，代表收斂不是一次到位，而是每輪淘汰一些、留下一些。

低擬真原型

紙本流程、對話腳本、Figma 線框。目的是快速驗證，不求漂亮。

綠野仙蹤法（Wizard of Oz）

聊天機器人特別適用：由真人在幕後扮演 AI 回覆，先驗證「對話設計」是否有效，再投資技術。

可用性測試

找 5 位使用者邊用邊說出想法（think-aloud），觀察卡住、猶豫、皺眉的時刻。

心理健康領域的額外檢查

危機轉介流程、隱私與資料保存、AI 角色的界線聲明，都要在測試前與專業人員一起確認。

CASE · 「睡前三分鐘」原型片段
嗨，今晚還醒著呀。想聊聊，還是只想有人陪一下？
想聊聊只想被聽丟個顏色就好
想聊聊
好。今天哪個時刻最讓你在意？不用完整，一個畫面就可以。
報告被組員說做得很爛
聽起來很不好受。這種感覺比較像「委屈」「丟臉」還是「生氣」？也可以都不是。
委屈丟臉生氣說不上來

第一輪測試發現（綠野仙蹤法，6 位學生）

  • 😊 「只想被聽」選項很受歡迎，學生說「第一次覺得不用被修好」。
  • 😊 情緒詞卡幫助 5/6 的人說出更精確的感受。
  • 😣 三題有點多，凌晨時只想回一題。
  • 😣 不確定對話會不會被諮商中心看到。

第二輪迭代：縮成一題、可選擇延伸；開場直接說明「對話只存在你的手機」。

05 · 練習

自己寫一句挑戰陳述與 HMW

填入你自己專案的內容，下方會即時組成課堂的兩個句型。

工作坊時間表（依課堂簡報整理）

20 分Discover　分享田野故事 → 畫張力與模式 → 紅色便利貼
10 分Define　😊😣 痛點地圖
10 分Define　挑戰陳述＋根本原因（綠色）
—Define　寫 HMW 問題（藍色）
15 分Develop　快速發想（黃色）→ 歸納概念主題（大張黃色）
—Deliver　用 DVF＋創新性評估概念

重點回顧

先問對問題

第一顆鑽石的價值在於避免「很有效率地解決錯的問題」。晚安小站的起點是「做 AI」，終點是「讓人安心說出感受」。

發散與收斂要分開

發想時不評判，評估時才用 DVF。兩件事混在一起，點子會在萌芽前就被否決。

每一步都留下可追溯的痕跡

紅 → 綠 → 藍 → 黃的顏色鏈，讓每個方案都能追溯回某個真實使用者的痛點。

流程不是直線

測試發現新問題時，可以回到 Discover 或 Define。雙鑽石是地圖，不是單行道。

Design Thinking · Double Diamond

A Design Thinking Handbook:
Walking a Mental-Health Chatbot Through the Double Diamond

Built from the class slides, with methods and tools filled in from outside sources. One case, "Good Night Station", runs through all four phases so you can watch a vague problem become a testable solution. Unfamiliar terms are explained in the glossary.

00 · Overview

From A (don't know) to B (do know)

Tim Brown, chief executive officer (CEO) of IDEO, describes design thinking as a human-centered approach to innovation that brings together people's needs, the possibilities of technology and the requirements for business success. The Double Diamond, introduced by the UK Design Council in 2004, breaks this into two diamonds and four phases. Each phase first diverges (opens up possibilities) and then converges (makes choices).

The first diamond is about designing the right thing: understanding the real problem and narrowing it into a How Might We (HMW) question. The second is about designing things right: finding and refining the solution. Tap a phase below to jump to it.

The bottom row shows what each phase hands on to the next. Inside the first diamond, research is compressed step by step: Insights → Themes → Opportunity areas → HMW.

The class's sticky-note color system

The slides give every step its own color. Anyone can see at a glance where the team is on the wall, and trace each idea back to the pain point it came from.

The case: "Good Night Station"

CASE · RUNS THROUGH THE WHOLE PAGE (FICTIONAL)

A university counselling centre wants a conversational agent that helps students handle everyday emotions. The starting brief is one line: "Students rarely come to counselling, so we want an artificial intelligence (AI) chatbot." That is point A, a vague client brief.

The class cites four design insights for mental-health conversational agents from Tseng & Liao (2025): structured support, emotional awareness & self-disclosure, emotional safety & personalization, and from tracking to reflection. This counts as secondary research, and you will see it confirm the team's own findings later on.

01 DISCOVER 20 min in class

Discover: understand people before solving anything

Goal: build a rich understanding of people's mental-health experiences. Outcome: explore the tensions and patterns within them.

CLASSThree steps from the slides
  • Share field stories: each person tells a real moment from their interviews or observations. The more concrete, the better.
  • Identify relationships and map tensions and patterns: which stories repeat? Where do they contradict each other?
  • Write keywords on red sticky notes: one idea per note, so they are easy to move and cluster later.
MORECommon methods the class didn't cover in depth
Rip the brief

Pull apart every assumption in the client brief. Is it true that students don't come to counselling? Is "an AI chatbot" a solution or a pre-decided answer?

Primary research

In-depth interviews, contextual observation, diary studies. Ask "What happened the last time…?" to get concrete experiences rather than opinions.

Secondary research

Literature, competitor analysis, statistics. The four Tseng & Liao insights belong here.

Empathy map

Sort one user's Says / Thinks / Does / Feels into four boxes. Gaps between what people say and what they do are often where tensions hide.

CASEField stories from Good Night Station
CASE · DISCOVER

The team interviewed 8 students and 2 counsellors. A few representative stories (example content):

  • An, a third-year student: can't sleep at 2 a.m. and wants to talk, but "texting a friend at this hour feels like a bother".
  • Jay, a graduate student: logged his mood with an app for a month. "The charts look nice, but I still don't know what's wrong with me."
  • A counsellor: first appointments have a two-to-three-week wait. "Many students only come once they've already hit breaking point."
  • Mia, a first-year: tried an AI chat. "It kept telling me to take deep breaths. I felt brushed off."

These three tensions line up with Tseng & Liao's "emotional safety & self-disclosure", "structured support & personalization" and "from tracking to reflection". When primary and secondary research agree, the direction is more trustworthy.

02 DEFINE 10 + 10 min in class + HMW

Define: narrow findings into a problem worth solving

The class converges in three steps: mark the pain points, ask why they happen, then rewrite them as an HMW question. This matches the Insights → Themes → Opportunity areas → HMW compression in the diagram.

CLASSStep 1: Pain point mapping (10 min)

Place emojis on the "moments" of the user's experience: 😊 positive moments (delight, flow, success) and 😣 negative moments (confusion, waiting, frustration, distrust). The point is to find the moments where needs go unmet.

More: this is a simplified customer journey map. The full version records actions, thoughts, emotions, touchpoints and opportunities for each moment.

CASE · AN'S NIGHT

Notice that "always there, no pressure" is itself a positive moment. The trouble lies in reply quality and the lack of meaning afterwards, which reminds the team not to throw out the chatbot entirely.

CLASSStep 2: Frame challenges (10 min, green notes)

For each negative pain point, ask why it happens, and write it in this format:

"The challenge is that [people] struggle to [unmet need / problem], because [root cause]."

More: use the 5 Whys to dig for root causes. Ask "why" up to five times until you reach a cause you can act on, rather than stopping at a surface symptom.

CASE · DIGGING INTO "GETS CANNED ADVICE 😣"
  • Why distrust? → The reply had nothing to do with what she said.
  • Why unrelated? → The bot didn't catch her real feeling, only "can't sleep".
  • Why didn't it catch it? → An couldn't put it into words herself and only shared the surface.
  • Why couldn't she? → She lacks words for emotions and worries about being judged if she says too much.
The challenge is that university students struggle to express how they really feel in low moments, because they lack the vocabulary to name emotions and aren't sure the listener can safely hold what they share.
The challenge is that students struggle to get help from their mood logs, because the tools show data without guiding them to connect events and feelings.
CLASSStep 3: Write your How Might We question (blue notes)
How might we help [people / stakeholder] to [fulfill unmet need / achieve a desired outcome] by addressing [root cause]?

The HMW format started at Procter & Gamble in the 1970s and was later popularized by IDEO. Each word has a job: How assumes it can be solved, Might allows many possibilities, and We signals working together.

Common mistake: a scope that is too broad or too narrow.

ScopeExampleProblem
Too broadHMW solve students' mental-health problems?No idea where to start
Too narrowHMW add ten more emotion buttons to the chatbot?Already assumes the solution
Just rightSee the case belowGives direction yet leaves room for many solutions
CASE · GOOD NIGHT STATION'S HMW
How might we help sleepless university students feel safe sorting out and expressing their feelings by addressing their lack of emotional vocabulary and fear of being judged?
How might we help students who log their moods see their own patterns by addressing tools that give data but no meaning?

The first diamond ends here. The team has moved from "build an AI chatbot" to two specific, people-centered questions. This is the final brief handed to the second diamond.

03 DEVELOP 15 min in class

Develop: go for quantity, then cluster

CLASSTwo steps
  • Rapid ideation: one idea per yellow sticky note, as many as possible for each HMW.
  • Group similar ideas into broader concept themes: use large yellow notes as theme headers. This is an affinity diagram.
MOREIDEO.org's seven brainstorming rules
1. Defer judgment
No "that's impossible" for now.
2. Encourage wild ideas
Outrageous ideas often lead to workable ones.
3. Build on others' ideas
Say "yes, and…" instead of "but".
4. Stay focused
Keep the HMW on the wall in view.
5. One conversation at a time
Every idea gets heard.
6. Be visual
Sketch it; drawings travel faster than words.
7. Go for quantity
Aim for 50 ideas in 15 minutes.
Extra: Crazy 8s
Eight sketches in eight minutes pushes you past your first idea.

04 DELIVER Evaluate → prototype → test → iterate

Deliver: pick what's worth building, build it, test it

CLASSFour evaluation criteria
Desirability

Does it meet real user needs or create value?

Feasibility

Can it be implemented with available resources and technology?

Viability

Is it sustainable for the organization or ecosystem?

Innovation

Does it introduce a new perspective or alternative approach?

More: the first three are IDEO's classic desirability, feasibility and viability (DVF) lenses, and the best solutions sit where all three overlap. The class adds Innovation as a fourth. Design thinking suggests starting with desirability and bringing in the other lenses later.

CASE · INTERACTIVE SCORING (DRAG THE SLIDERS)

The team condensed the four themes into three concepts and scored each criterion from 1 to 5. The scores are examples; adjust them to your own judgment.

MOREPrototyping and testing: build, test, iterate

The right half of the second diamond shows several rounds of "build, test, iterate". Converging isn't a single step: each round drops some ideas and keeps others.

Low-fidelity prototypes

Paper flows, conversation scripts, Figma wireframes. The aim is fast validation, not polish.

Wizard of Oz

Especially suited to chatbots: a person plays the AI behind the scenes, so you validate the conversation design before investing in the technology.

Usability testing

Have 5 users think aloud while using it, and watch for moments where they get stuck, hesitate or frown.

Extra checks for mental health

Confirm crisis referral, privacy and data retention, and the AI's stated boundaries with professionals before testing.

Round 1 findings (Wizard of Oz, 6 students)

  • 😊 "Just listen" was a favorite. One student said "For once I didn't feel like I needed fixing."
  • 😊 Emotion word cards helped 5 of 6 describe their feelings more precisely.
  • 😣 Three prompts felt like too many; at 2 a.m. people wanted to answer just one.
  • 😣 Unsure whether the counselling centre could see the conversation.

Key takeaways

Ask the right question first

The first diamond stops you from efficiently solving the wrong problem. Good Night Station started at "build an AI" and ended at "help people feel safe expressing their feelings".

Keep diverging and converging apart

Don't judge while ideating; bring in DVF only when evaluating. Mix the two and ideas get killed before they can grow.

Leave a traceable trail

The red → green → blue → yellow chain lets every solution be traced back to a real user's pain point.

The process isn't a straight line

When testing uncovers a new problem, go back to Discover or Define. The Double Diamond is a map, not a one-way street.
